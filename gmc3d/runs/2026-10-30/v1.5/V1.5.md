# v1.5.0 results (30 Oct 2026): curved meshes, currents on any surface, mesh surface currents

Everything below comes from `validate.log`, `tests.log`, `currents.log`,
`rerun.log`, `openmc_bugs.log` and `probes/` in this folder. 2M histories
per validate run. OpenMC 0.16 ran in 400 batches. The pass rule is as
before: no tally chi2 with p < 0.001, and no single value with |z| > 3.29.

## What is new

- **`CylindricalMesh` and `SphericalMesh` (V1-TAL-11).**
  - Flights stop at every curved bin wall:
    - r edges are cylinders or spheres;
    - phi edges are half-planes through the axis;
    - theta edges are cones;
    - z edges are planes.
  - Each piece of a flight is scored in the bin that holds its midpoint.
  - Balls use the v1.1 rule unchanged. A ball's room is the exact distance
    to the nearest bin wall.
- **Surface currents on any surface (V1-TAL-12).** This covers
  reflective, white and periodic walls, and surfaces inside universes and
  lattices.
  - Reflective and white walls score exactly 0, as OpenMC does (OpenMC
    scores +w then -w).
  - A periodic crossing is scored once, on the partner wall the particle
    comes in through.
  - Inner surfaces are signed in the universe's own frame.
- **`MeshSurfaceFilter` (V1-TAL-13).** It gives the current through each
  voxel face, with the in and out directions kept separate, in OpenMC's
  bin order. Where it is present, balls are shrunk to fit inside one
  voxel, so the answer stays exact but balls save less time there.

## Three places where OpenMC 0.16 is wrong (openmc_bugs.log)

Each was found when our new tally disagreed with OpenMC. Each was then
settled by an exact answer or a conservation law, and in each case ours
is right. These cases are now checked by unit tests instead of against
OpenMC, and the validation problems avoid them.

1. **SphericalMesh theta edge at exactly pi/2.** The test case is a pure
   absorber, where the exact uncollided flux per bin can be computed.
   - Ours: chi2/dof 0.82 against the exact answer.
   - OpenMC: chi2/dof 15,097, with max|z| 313.
   - Other cone angles are fine in OpenMC (chi2/dof 0.3-1.0 at 80
     batches).
   - The first sphere run (`curved_mesh.log`, chi2/dof 88) used a pi/2
     edge. That is how this was found.
2. **Currents on surfaces inside a moved universe.** The probe is an
   absorbing sphere with no source inside, so the net current into it
   must equal minus its absorption.

   | where the sphere sits | OpenMC current / absorption | ours current / absorption |
   |---|---|---|
   | in the root, a plain universe, or a 1x1 lattice | -0.132 / 0.132 (right) | -0.133 / 0.133 |
   | 3x3 lattice | **+0.169** / 0.493 | -0.4922 / 0.4922 |
   | translated universe | **-0.290** / 0.312 | -0.3143 / 0.3142 |

   OpenMC gets it right only when the universe's frame matches the root's.
3. **Rotational periodic walls: the sign of the current.**
   - In a beam test, a particle leaving once through the x = 0 wall gets
     -0.996 from OpenMC and +0.995 from us. Leaving twice gets -0.06 from
     OpenMC and +1.93 from us.
   - OpenMC signs the turned direction against the normal of the wall the
     particle left, which is the current of neither wall.
   - Translational walls agree in both codes (tilted passes).

## Speed

- **A slowdown found and fixed.**
  - The first build made old problems slower: cask by 43% and hexcore by
    22%.
  - Bisected cause: every array added to the shared tally and geometry
    records costs time on every kernel call that receives them, whether or
    not it is used.
  - The fix keeps the new mesh data in a separate record that only the new
    kernels receive.
  - After the fix, at 200k x 4 histories, best of 3:

    | problem | v1.5 | v1.4.1 |
    |---|---|---|
    | cask | 19.73 s | 20.0-20.3 s |
    | hexcore | 28.32 s | 27.2-28.9 s |

- **Validate times**, plain MC / exact balls in seconds:

  | problem | v1.5 | v1.4.1 | why |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | sphere | 33.8 / 15.5 | 20.2 / 13.1 | new spherical-mesh tally |
  | curved | 170.5 / 129.3 | 88.3 / 67.7 | two new curved-mesh tallies |
  | slab | 36.4 / 54.7 | 33.3 / 35.5 | mesh-face tally caps the balls |
  | nested | 89.8 / 104.5 | 86.3 / 94.1 | new mesh-face tally |
  | others | within ±10% or faster | | |

- The cost of a curved mesh lies in its tally, not in the transport. The
  general speed question stays with the Speed track.

## Unit tests: 156 pass (18 new, tests.log)

- The curved walk agrees with brute-force binning on four meshes.
- A ball's room keeps it inside one bin, and is tight for centres inside
  the mesh.
- The theta edge at pi/2 agrees with the exact answer.
- Current conservation holds on inner spheres in the root, in a shifted
  universe and in a lattice.
- The rotational beam tests give the expected signs.
- White and reflective walls give 0.
- Mesh faces agree with brute force.
- On the slab, the mesh-face balance holds, and balls and plain MC agree.
- Refusals: MeshSurfaceFilter combined with another filter, on a curved
  mesh, and MeshBornFilter.

## Limits (all in GAPS.md)

- Refused:
  - MeshSurfaceFilter on a curved mesh;
  - MeshSurfaceFilter combined with other filters.
- Curved meshes are not written to vtk files or flux maps.
- Outside the mesh, the curved room is a conservative bound, so balls
  there are smaller than they need to be (still exact).
- With a MeshSurfaceFilter, balls are capped to one voxel. Keeping full
  size balls there would be a model change.
- Mesh faces lying on reflective, white or periodic walls are not counted.
- For the three OpenMC errors above, our answers rest on exact and
  conservation checks, not on OpenMC.
